# Research options for reliable fruit mapping

Reviewed 4 October 2026. This is a research brief, not a measured improvement or a production change.

The engineering target should be at least 80% of independently identified physical fruit yielding a trustworthy map. Keep the existing 80% surface-coverage requirement separate from that acceptance rate. A raw tracker ID is not a physical-fruit denominator. Neither additional images nor a plausible complete mesh establish that previously unseen peel has been inspected.

## Recommended order

1. **Measure the limiting failure before choosing a replacement.** Count physical fruit traversing a predefined inspection region, and classify each failure as insufficient visible surface, blur/glare, identity ambiguity, mask failure, pose-registration failure, or fusion failure. Validate accepted maps using independently checked texture correspondences and duplicate-defect consistency. Keep capture eligibility and coverage thresholds fixed across comparisons. This protocol is an engineering recommendation; the cited papers do not report a comparable 80%-of-all-fruit mapping acceptance metric.

2. **Use signed, full 3D poses and a graph of reliable observations.** Fruit 340's reversal and tumbling violate the present positive rolling and monotonic-longitude assumptions. Register overlapping keyframes, retain reliable direct measurements even if an intermediate adjacent match fails, refine poses jointly, and fuse the union of observed surface in one fruit coordinate frame. Require identity continuity, robust correspondences, forward/reverse consistency, and loop checks. An unresolved component remains unregistered. This proposal adapts the memory frames and pose graph of [BundleTrack (IROS 2021)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.00516) and [BundleSDF (CVPR 2023)](https://bundlesdf.github.io/). Those methods were evaluated on generic object benchmarks and use RGB-D, so their reported performance is not citrus evidence and they are not direct RGB-only replacements.

3. **Benchmark a deeper RGB registration fallback on the hardest fruit.** [VGGSfM (CVPR 2024)](https://vggsfm.github.io/) extracts tracks across images, estimates cameras jointly and refines them using bundle adjustment. Unlike chaining independent pairs, this can use agreement across multiple frames. It was evaluated on CO3D, IMC Phototourism and ETH3D. More directly, [PhenoCitrus (2025)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304423825005862) applied masked RGB rotation videos, VGGSfM, 3D Gaussian Splatting and a mesh to controlled citrus phenotyping. The paper reports surface-area/volume agreement of R-squared above 0.9 and MAPE below 5%. It used a motorized platform, a lighting cabin and diffused LED panels. That supports a controlled-capture trial and an experimental registration fallback; it does not establish reliable seams, defect mapping or conveyor throughput for this video. A smooth reconstruction can fill holes without observing them.

4. **Remove glare at acquisition.** Diffuse the lighting, shield changing ambient light, lock exposure/white balance after calibration, and test orthogonal polarisers on light and camera. [Nguyen-Do-Trong et al. (2016)](https://lirias.kuleuven.be/bitstream/123456789/548332/1/Manuscript.pdf) demonstrated cross-polarisation on aubergine and two apple cultivars, removing specular contributions in 450-1000 nm imagery and improving reported signal-to-noise ratio by 1.1-3 times depending on wavelength. The adaptation to this mandarin RGB texture matcher is an inference. Polarisation reduces available light, so sufficient illumination and short exposure must be maintained; retest defect detection and colour grading after changing capture conditions.

5. **Guarantee sufficient rotation while the fruit is completely visible.** [Albiol et al. (2022)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9323781/) require visible skin texture and substantial overlap; they report using 20-40 degrees between views and industrial examples with 14-16 views spanning roughly 360 degrees. Their conveyor independently adjusts rotation and transport, with about one full turn during a one-second transit. They explicitly warn that fruit can slip, so encoder speed alone cannot establish actual fruit rotation. Their 2088-orange classification experiment and macro F1 are not a mapping acceptance test. Engineering constraints are omega*T >= 360 degrees and omega/F <= theta_max, where T is fully visible residence time, F is captured FPS and omega is measured fruit angular speed. For the current 20-degree pair bound and 30 FPS, omega must stay below 600 degrees/second; at one turn/second the increment is about 12 degrees/frame and at least one second of fully visible residence is needed. Add margin, separation between fruit and short enough exposure to preserve peel features. More FPS helps overlap and blur handling, but does not make a nonrotating fruit reveal its back.

6. **Add another calibrated view or change orientation when rotation cannot expose enough peel.** A second oblique camera or a controlled second orientation can observe persistent blind regions and provide independent pose constraints. Register those views into the same fruit frame before counting coverage. [Albiol et al. (2021)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8004661/) explicitly state that many views do not guarantee whole-surface observation and that uniform texture makes rotation unobservable. Their spheroid method was evaluated on real oranges, mandarins and tomatoes. Multi-camera improvement on this conveyor is an engineering inference that requires a capture trial, not a quoted success guarantee.

## Newer fruit-specific research and its practical limits

[Agri-MVPS (WACV HARVEST-Vision Workshop, March 2026)](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2026W/HARVEST-Vision/html/Zuniga-Santana_A_Multi-View_Photometric_Stereo_Pipeline_for_Specular_3D_Fruit_Reconstruction_WACVW_2026_paper.html) combines polarised HDR capture, calibrated multiple light directions and photometric normals with a neural implicit reconstruction. It includes cherries, tomato, strawberry, nutshell and tangerine, addressing low texture and strong specular reflections. It uses 20 turntable viewpoints and 3120 RAW images per fruit. Reported acquisition takes about 1 hour 40 minutes, with further hours of preprocessing and minutes of reconstruction. Most multi-view comparisons use its full 20-view reconstruction as the reference; independent sprayed geometry is obtained for one cherry. This is useful evidence for capture design and offline reference-data generation. It is not a viable direct replacement for a high-throughput RGB conveyor pipeline.

Shape completion, painted/particle-coated fruit or projected fixed-light patterns should not be counted as successful peel-defect inspection: completed texture is unobserved, coating alters defects, and a camera-fixed pattern does not rotate with the peel. These may help geometric calibration, but require separate original-colour observations for defect inspection.

## Suggested decision trial

Run a fixed, independently labelled sample of complete physical-fruit passages with current capture. Compare the existing mapper, a signed pose/observation-graph variant and a feature-track fallback using identical views and masks. Report surface coverage, verified reprojection error, seam/defect consistency, identity contamination and runtime, plus failure counts. Then repeat capture for the same fruit with diffuse/cross-polarised lighting and controlled rotation. A capture-limited failure cannot be corrected by relaxing registration confidence. Choose the combination that increases trustworthy accepted fruit, then verify it on a held-out pass. The reviewed literature does not establish that the current source video can reach the user's target without changing capture.
